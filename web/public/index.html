The problem

The pin starts the agent hundreds of metres from the house.

Commercial geocoders drop an Indian address such as “near Hanuman temple, behind ration shop, 2nd cross” at the pincode or locality centre. Agents spend the day searching, and one visit in four ends “address not traceable”.

The right answer already exists: the GPS point where an agent actually met the borrower. It sits in the visit log and never flows back into the address record.

₹150 to 400cost of every productive visit

Our solution

Combine every weak clue by how reliable it is.

The old pin becomes one clue among several. We trust each clue in proportion to its reliability, drop any that clash with the rest, and tell the field team honestly how sure we are.

1

Clean the visits

Each noisy GPS trail becomes one trusted point with a role and an uncertainty. Reused photos and repeated stops are down-weighted.

2

Read the address

A multilingual parser handles English, Hinglish, Kanglish, Devanagari and Kannada, with typos, and finds locality, street numbers and landmarks.

3

Fuse the clues

Own visit, same-street neighbours, the street-number grid, landmarks and the old pin are combined into one pin.

4

Say how sure

Calibrated 50% and 90% circles, a confidence tier with a suggested action, and offline landmark directions.

Under the hood

A probabilistic evidence-fusion model, not a black box.

Four ideas do the work. Each is small enough to audit, and each is tested on its own in the repository.

1. Fuse clues by reliability

x̂ = Σ wiμi / Σ wi,   wi = 1 / σi2

Every clue gives a guess μi and an error scale σi that depends on how many neighbours back it and how tightly they agree. The spread is inflated when clues disagree.

Robust gate. The clue with the largest conflict ri = |μi − x̂−i| / √(σi2 + 1/W−i) is dropped when ri > 3.2, up to three times. Two or more agreeing visits are never gated away.

2. Street numbers are coordinates

(x, y) = a + B·(cross, main)   per locality

A kernel-weighted local-linear regression, re-weighted to resist wrongly placed neighbours, predicts streets nobody has visited. Its predictive spread includes leverage, so a corner of the grid is trusted less than the middle.

This is the biggest gain after the visits themselves: removing it adds about 38 m to the median.

3. Fit without the answer key

min Σ Huber120 m( x̂hidden − visit pin )

Each visited address has its own visit hidden and is predicted from everything else, so 1,134 addresses act as labels and the 100 field-verified ones stay clean. Tuning on half the localities and scoring on the other half gives 116 to 126 m, against 113 m in sample.

4. Honest circles

rp = Qp( e / spread ) · spread

Split conformal prediction, with a finite-sample quantile, per group (own visit, no visit, no locality in text). Radii are calibrated on data the model never saw and checked on different data.

Coverage is marginal, not guaranteed for every small stratum; the report says which strata are too small to conclude anything.

Why not just use machine learning?

Every method gets the same clues. Median (blue) and 90th percentile (gold) error in metres.

Gradient-boosted trees on the parsed address fields are a strong competitor on the median. The fusion model is better in the tail, gives an explainable pin and a calibrated spread, and learns from a new visit without retraining. Equal weighting is worse than the best single clue; weighting by reliability is what halves the error.

Python, NumPy, pandas, scikit-learn, deployed on Vercel.
